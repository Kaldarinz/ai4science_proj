## Verdict: four own-data claims, all on pulse fluence

The article varies pulse fluence (J/cm2) in two media and reports the size dependence from its own
TEM measurements. Recorded claims:

- `538366-c1` (p.3, decrease, water): "As it is shown the average size decreases with increasing
  laser fluence." The intermediate-fluence series 7-35 J/cm2 (14, 13, 13, 12, 8 nm, Fig. 3).
- `538366-c2` (p.4, increase, water): "It is observed that when laser fluence exceeds a certain
  value (above 35 J/cm2), the average size and concentration begin to increase ...". The
  high-fluence series 53-88 J/cm2 (11, 13, 14 nm, Fig. 4).
- `538366-c3` (p.4, non_monotonic, water): "From the above discussion, the average size decreases
  as the laser fluence increases until it reaches the critical size then it begins to increase
  again as shown in the reported data in Table 1 and Fig. 5."
- `538366-c4` (p.8, non_monotonic, aqueous): "The same trend was noticed in 40% ethanol solution
  but the critical size was obtained at higher fluence (7 nm at 53 J/cm2) due to higher
  agglomeration."

Why three water claims rather than one: claims_spec section 2 allows one claim per distinct
`(material, medium_class, variable, direction, basis)`. The article states a decrease over one
fluence range, an increase over another, and the combined non-monotonic dependence; these are
three distinct directions and are kept separately so that a valley-shaped series can agree with
the combined claim while disagreeing with each single-range claim.

Why the non-monotonic quote is the p.4 sentence and not the earlier p.3 opening sentence: the p.3
sentence "At low laser fluence the gold nanoparticle size decreases when the laser fluence
increases while at high fluences region, the gold nanoparticle size increases when the laser
fluence increases" is the first results-section occurrence of the combined statement, but the p.4
sentence is the explicit own-data summary that points to Table 1 and Fig. 5. Both are in the
results section, so section 2's "quote the results section" is satisfied either way; the p.4
sentence was chosen as the self-contained summary. The abstract (p.1) restates the same
non-monotonic dependence and is therefore not a separate claim.

Why the ethanol claim is separate: the medium is a 60:40 water:ethanol "aqueous solution" (p.5),
i.e. water with an additive, so `medium_class = aqueous` and the tuple differs from the water
claims. The quote is the single p.8 sentence because the antecedent of "the same trend" is the
sentence that begins on p.7 ("As the energy increases above this" / p.8 "value the nanoparticles
begin to agglomerate again and the size increases") and a quote must be found on its own page;
the antecedent is recorded in `conditions`.

## Rejected sentences and reasons

- p.1 abstract "This study addresses the influence of laser parameters such as laser fluence,
  laser wavelength as well as focusing condition of laser beam on the size and morphology of the
  gold nanoparticles ..." — announces the study, names three laser parameters, states no
  direction; the section 2 condition for an unnamed "laser parameters" dependence (only
  fluence/energy/power/intensity varied) is not met because wavelength and focusing also vary.
- p.1 and p.7 "The control of the gold nanoparticles size and hence colloidal properties is
  possible using laser parameters such as laser fluence and wavelength as well as varying the
  focusing conditions." — general, no direction, several laser parameters named; not a claim.
- p.3 "From TEM micrographs it is found that, as laser power increases, the particles become
  homogenous and have spherical shape, also the concentration increases until it takes critical
  size at 35 J/cm2, the particles appear as diffused particles." — morphology and concentration,
  not size; "laser power" is used loosely for fluence but no size change is stated.
- p.4 "The small red-shift in the plasmon peak assures the decrease in the average size of gold
  nanoparticles." — optical proxy for size, not a statement of size versus fluence.
- p.4 "As the laser power increases, the plasmon peak becomes narrower indicating that the
  particles have a homogenous distribution." — plasmon width, not size.
- p.5 "The peak width decreases with a small blue-shift in the plasmon peak due to the small
  increase in the size of the gold nanoparticles." — optical property, no laser variable.
- p.5 "The average size of the prepared gold nanoparticles is 9, 13, 11, 10, 10, 9, 8, 7 and 9 nm
  at laser fluences 4, 7, 11, 14, 18, 35, 53 and 88 J/cm2 respectively." — values only; a
  dependence the reader infers from values is data, not a claim (section 2). Also internally
  inconsistent (see below).
- p.5 "The nanoparticles critical size was obtained in 40% ethanol at 53 J/cm2 while that prepared
  in deionised water reaches critical size at 35 J/cm2." — locates the minimum in fluence in two
  media; states no direction of size change with fluence, so it is a medium comparison, not a
  claim. The ethanol dependence itself is recorded as `538366-c4` from the p.8 conclusion.
- p.6 "The average size of gold nanoparticles prepared at 532 nm is smaller than that prepared at
  1064 nm." (twice) — wavelength, not one of the four variables.
- p.7 "The same behavior was noticed when using a larger focal length of 10 cm but the average
  size of the obtained gold nanoparticles increases as the focal length increases." — focal
  length, not one of the four variables.
- p.7 "The fluence decreases with increasing focal length, as a result, the average size increases
  and the yield of the obtained particles also increases." — a fluence-size statement, but the
  same `(Au, water, fluence, decrease, own_data)` tuple as `538366-c1`; a restatement, not a new
  claim. Same for "at focal length (f = 20 cm), the average size of gold nanoparticles is larger
  than that obtained at focal lengths 5 and 10 cm as the laser fluence became smaller (18 J/cm2)".
- p.7 "The average size of the gold nanoparticles prepared at 'below focusing' decreases down to
  7 nm." and the above/at/below-focus sizes 8, 9, 7 nm (Table 3) — focusing position, and single
  values; the fluence differences are not the stated cause of the size change in that sentence.
- p.7 conclusion "As the laser fluence increases, the size of gold nanoparticles prepared in
  de-ionized water decreases until they reached their critical size (8 nm at 35 J/cm2) below which
  the particle became insensitive to the laser energy." — the "insensitive to the laser energy"
  clause would read as a `no_effect` claim, but it is contradicted by the immediately following
  sentence ("As the energy increases above this value the nanoparticles begin to agglomerate again
  and the size increases") and by the recorded non-monotonic claim; it is a garbled restatement of
  the decrease branch, not a separate no-effect claim.
- p.8 "Smaller size distribution and yield was obtained at shorter wavelength of 532 nm." —
  wavelength and distribution width, not size versus fluence.
- p.8 "The focal length and the position of the samples both have a notable affect on the shape
  and size distribution of the gold nanoparticles." — focal length and position, no direction.
- p.8 "Small size and narrow distribution of the gold nanoparticles prepared by laser ablation can
  be controlled by optimizing the laser parameters and focusing conditions." — general, no
  direction, several parameters.
- Figure and table captions (Figs. 2-11, Tables 1-4) — captions alone are excluded by section 2.

## Textual inconsistencies (for the curator, not extraction decisions)

- p.5 lists nine sizes ("9, 13, 11, 10, 10, 9, 8, 7 and 9 nm") for eight fluences (4, 7, 11, 14,
  18, 35, 53, 88 J/cm2), and Table 2 gives 13, 11, 10, 10, 9, 8, 7, 9 nm at 4, 7, 11, 14, 18, 35,
  53, 70 J/cm2. The sentence's leading "9" and its last fluence (88 versus 70 J/cm2) disagree with
  Table 2.
- Table 1 header reads "laser fluence (J/m 2)" while the body values (7-88) and the text are
  J/cm2.
- p.7 conclusion "below which the particle became insensitive to the laser energy" contradicts the
  next sentence (see above).
- p.6 gives the focusing-series pulse width as 6 ns and 10 Hz with 50 mJ, while the p.2 setup
  states 10 ns and 20 Hz; the focusing series is a separate experiment, so this is a reporting
  inconsistency rather than a contradiction of the fluence series.
