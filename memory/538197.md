## Verdict: no claim

The article varies wavelength, beam profile and scanning speed. Fluence is one stated value
(1.36 J/cm2 at the fundamental, p.2) and pulse energy is held identical between Gaussian and
Bessel at each wavelength, so no fluence/energy/power/intensity series exists and no sentence
states a size dependence on one of those four variables.

Recorded no-claim quote (p.2): "Additionally, PLAL allows for precise control over NP size by
adjusting laser parameters or the liquid composition, making it a versatile tool for NP
synthesis [ 37,38]." It is the closest sentence because it links size to laser parameters, but
it is cited to [37,38], states no direction, names the liquid composition next to the laser,
and the article's varied laser parameters are wavelength, beam profile and scanning speed, so
the claims_spec section 2 condition for an unnamed "laser parameters" dependence fails.

## Rejected sentences and reasons

- p.2 "The effects of laser parameters such as wavelength, pulse duration, repetition rate, and
  energy per pulse on nanoparticle (NP) properties have been well studied." — names energy per
  pulse but states no size dependence and no direction ("NP properties"), and is a general
  literature remark.
- p.2 "although the redistribution of energy into concentric rings can reduce the on-axis
  fluence and occasionally induce additional heating in the surrounding medium" — beam-shaping
  mechanism, no size statement.
- p.2 "All experiments were performed at the maximum available pulse energy for each
  wavelength after harmonic generation." — a fixed setting, not a varied variable; no size
  statement.
- p.1 abstract "reduced average particle size - particularly at longer wavelengths - due to
  their extended focal depth and quasi-uniform axial intensity profile" — attributes size to
  beam profile and wavelength, not to fluence/energy/power/intensity.
- p.4 "At 1064 nm ... the Bessel beam results in slightly narrower distributions and smaller
  average particle sizes." — beam-profile comparison at fixed energy.
- p.5 "showing a clear variation in the average particle size and distribution width with
  changing scan velocity" and "When the scanning speed is increased to 1.5 mm/s, the Gaussian
  ablation results again in a wide distribution with the presence of significantly larger
  particles, likely due to reduced pulse overlap and less efficient energy coupling." —
  scanning speed is not fluence, energy, power or intensity (section 5); the energy-coupling
  clause is a mechanism, not a stated dependence.
- p.6 "This improvement is attributed to the Bessel beam's long focal depth and ring-structured
  energy delivery, which maintain high fluence over an extended axial range and promote stable
  material removal." — mechanism for ablation efficiency, no size dependence on fluence.
- p.6 "A recent study on Bessel-beam ablation of silver targets compared different pulse
  energies and reported spherical nanoparticles with an SPR peak around 400 nm [40]." — silver,
  cited, and reports an SPR peak, not a size dependence.
- p.6 conclusions "the Bessel beam profile significantly improves NP generation compared to the
  Gaussian profile in terms of particle size, distribution, and yield" and "At 266 nm ...
  the Bessel profile produced a higher NP concentration and smaller average particle size
  compared to the Gaussian beam" — beam-profile and wavelength comparisons.
- Figure captions (Figs. 1-7) — captions alone are excluded by section 2.
