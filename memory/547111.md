## Verdict

No claim. One `no_claims.csv` row quotes the p.2 SPR sentence.

## Why the p.2 SPR sentence is not a claim

1. The dependent quantity is the height and width of the SPR peak at 520 nm — an optical response of the
   colloid, not nanoparticle size. claims_spec §2 requires a statement that *size* changes or does not
   change with fluence, energy, power or intensity.
2. The sentence does not name size at all; the colloid's size in that experiment is the single reported
   distribution 40-60 nm over 25-120 nm (Figure 2).
3. The laser energy here is written in mJ/pulse, i.e. `energy` under claims_spec §5, and it is genuinely
   varied by the authors (0.16 vs 0.8 mJ/pulse), so the rejection rests on the *dependent variable*, not on
   the variable name.

## Rejected sentences and reasons

- p.2: "The height and the width of the SPR peak at 520 nm were dependent upon the energy of the laser
  pulses." and "At 0.16 mJ/pulse the height and width at 520 nm were lower and less broadened in comparison
  to the case of the ablation performed at 0.8 mJ/pulse." — optical response, not size (quoted above as the
  closest sentence).
- p.2: "This was consistent with the results of Mafune et al., 7b who have shown that the nanoparticles can
  only be produced above a threshold laser power and that their number density increases proportionally with
  the laser power." — `cited`, and the dependent quantity is number density, not size; a production threshold
  is not a size dependence.
- p.2: "It should be noted that a 532-nm Nd:YAG laser 5d,7b had significantly larger energy per pulse
  (90 mJ/pulse), which should influence the absolute abundance of the produced particles." — compares with
  cited work and concerns abundance, not size; no direction for size.
- p.2: "TEM micrographs revealed that the nanoparticles were nearly spherical and their average sizes
  decreased with an increase in the CD concentration with a very sharp particle size distribution (Figure 3)."
  — the varied quantity is cyclodextrin concentration, an additive, not a laser variable.
- p.3: "β-CD was most effective in reducing the size and dispersion of the gold nanoparticles, followed by
  γ-CD and α-CD." — CD type, not a laser variable (the same reading as for medium/type arguments elsewhere).
- p.3: "It should be noted that the laser ablation in the presence of 100 mM SDS led to much larger 4.6-8 nm
  particles with the dispersion of 5 nm fwhm." — cited surfactant comparison, no laser variable.
- p.3: "the nature of the perthiolated CD (α, β, or γ) was also reported to affect the average particle size
  ranging from 2 to 7 nm" — cited, chemical route, no laser variable.
- p.4: "The resulting nanoparticles were much smaller and more stable with increase in the CD concentration,
  implying that hydrophobic interactions between gold nanoparticles and CD molecules were of sufficient
  strength to prevent agglomeration" and "the nanoparticle size and dispersion decreased with increasing CD
  concentrations" — CD concentration again.
- p.4 conclusion, p.5 (reproducibility, homogeneous reduction, stabilizers, outlook) — no laser variable.
- Figure 1/2/3/4/5 captions and axis labels — excluded by claims_spec §2; Figure 3's "The laser energy was
  0.8 J/pulse" is a caption and also states a fixed setting.
