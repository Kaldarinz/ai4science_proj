# 538537 — Wender et al., Nanoscale 3 (2011) 1240–1245

Synthesis of gold nanoparticles by laser ablation of an Au foil inside and outside ionic liquids.
One main PDF (`10.1039_c0nr00786b.pdf`, 6 pp), no supplement.

## Evidence locations

- p.2 §2.2: single laser setting — 1064 nm Nd:YAG (Quanta-Ray GCR-170), "a fluence of 20 mJ cm-2 per
  pulse", 8.5 ns FWHM, 5 Hz, 6000 pulses, 10 min, 10 cm lens, target displaced from focus, elliptical
  spot 0.73 x 0.43 mm. Pulse energy "from about 18 mJ up to 540 mJ" is the laser's capability, not a
  varied experimental parameter.
- p.2 §2.2: the two configurations — Au foil inside the IL vs outside the IL (above the IL/air
  interface), cell slid parallel to its longer edge.
- p.2 §2.3: TEM histograms from >200 particles, spherical-shape assumption.
- p.3 Table 1: lognormal-fit mean and SD per IL and position — BMI.BF4 22.0/17.5 inside, 12.6/8.3
  outside; BMI.PF6 8.1/4.3 inside, 9.0/3.8 outside; BMI.N(CN)2 flower-like inside, 6.4/2.7 outside;
  (BCN)MI.NTf2 5.2 and 20.5 (1.1 and 2.1) inside, 9.1/3.8 outside.
- p.3–p.5: mechanism discussion (IL/air interface nucleation, anion coordination ability, surface
  composition and ion orientation, surface tension 0.0436/0.0488/0.0486 N m-1 at 298 K).
- p.5 conclusions: size and shape "depend mainly on the nature of the IL counter-anion and where the
  ablation is performed"; outside-IL mean diameters 5.4–12.6 nm.

## Verdict

No claim. The article varies no laser parameter: fluence is one fixed value (20 mJ/cm2), and the
varied factors are the IL anion and the foil position. One `no_claims.csv` row, quote from p.4.

## Rejected sentences and reasons

- p.1 "Additionally, laser ablation inside liquid matrices allows for further interactions with laser
  pulses, which can reshape, fragmentize or combine NPs." — names laser pulses but no fluence, energy,
  power or intensity and no direction; mechanism statement (claims_spec §2).
- p.3 "In the case of the larger AuNPs produced by laser ablation inside (BCN)MI$NTf2, a fragmentation
  process may have occurred, changing the AuNPs size and size distribution, consequently increasing the
  density of NPs." — mechanism, no laser variable, no direction.
- p.4 "The large size distribution can be easily diminished since the fragmentation process resulting in
  smaller AuNPs can be achieved by further laser ablation." — the varied quantity is the amount of
  irradiation (more pulses), not a per-pulse laser variable.
- p.4 "Additionally, a reduction of the particle size by further laser irradiation increased the
  stability of the AuNPs." — quoted in `no_claims.csv` as the closest sentence; same reason, plus it is
  attributed to ref. 24 (cited).
- p.4 "As cited above, a previous study has described Au laser ablation inside the ILs EMI$BF4, BMI$BF4
  and OMI$BF4 with laser pulses of 1024 and 532 nm." — wavelength, cited, no size dependence stated.
- p.4 "This is in contrast with what has been observed in the synthesis of NPs in ILs by the hydrogen
  reduction of inorganic salt precursors, where a linear increase in the NPs size with the IL anion
  molecular volume was achieved." — anion molecular volume, not a laser variable.
- p.4 "Comparing the anion effects on the AuNPs after laser ablation inside these two ILs, BMI$PF6
  generated smaller NPs than BMI$BF4." — medium (anion), not a laser variable.
- p.5 "A correlation was observed between AuNPs size and IL surface chemical composition, rather than
  surface tension and viscosity, which were secondary parameters." — medium property.
- p.5 "In summary, the size and shape of the nanoparticles prepared by laser ablation of Au foils depend
  mainly on the nature of the IL counter-anion and where the ablation is performed, i.e., with the Au
  foil placed inside or outside the ionic liquid." — medium and geometry, not a laser variable.
- p.5 "In these cases, irregular spherical-shaped AuNPs of mean diameters from 5.4 to 12.6 nm were
  obtained." — values at one setting, not a claim.
- p.1 abstract "The size and shape of the prepared NPs were related to where NP nucleation and growth
  occurred, i.e., at the IL surface or within the IL." — geometry/mechanism.
- Figure and table captions (Fig. 1–5, Table 1) — excluded by claims_spec §2.

## Keyword sweep (normalized pages)

`fluence` — only the single 20 mJ/cm2 setting (p.2). `energy` — the laser's 18–540 mJ pulse-energy
capability (p.2) and the kinetic energy of ejected atoms/clusters (p.1, p.4, p.5). `power` — none.
`intensity` — none. `irradiance` — none. No W/cm2 or GW/cm2 value anywhere. `pulse` — the fixed
duration/energy/pulse-count settings, "further interactions with laser pulses" (p.1), "laser pulses of
1024 and 532 nm" (p.4), "evaporated from the Au foil by the laser pulses" (p.4).

## Textual inconsistencies for the curator

- p.4 "In our case, 1264 nm ablation into BMI$BF4" against the 1064 nm setting of p.2 (likely a typo).
- p.4 "laser pulses of 1024 and 532 nm" for ref. 24 against the usual 1064/532 nm pair (as written).
- p.1 abstract gives "a diameter range of 5 to 20 nm" for all four ILs, while Table 1 reports 22.0 nm
  (SD 17.5) inside BMI.BF4 and flower-like ~50 nm inside BMI.N(CN)2; the abstract range describes the
  spherical particles only.
- p.5 conclusions give outside-IL mean diameters "from 5.4 to 12.6 nm", while Table 1 lists 6.4, 9.0,
  9.1 and 12.6 nm outside; 5.4 nm is not in Table 1 (the 5.2 nm component inside (BCN)MI.NTf2 is the
  nearest value).
