# 537505 — Bao et al., J. Colloid Interface Sci. 489 (2017) 92–99

"Ultrathin tin oxide layer-wrapped gold nanoparticles induced by laser ablation in solutions and
their enhanced performances". Main PDF 8 pp, supplied SI 4 pp.

## Evidence locations (laser and size)

- p.2, Experimental: single laser setting — "A Nd: YAG laser (1064 nm in wavelength, 10 Hz in
  frequency, and 10 ns in pulse width) was used to vertically irradiate the target for 10 min";
  "The laser power was 60 mJ per pulse and the laser spot size on the target was approximately
  2 mm with a focusing lens of 150 mm focal length." No second setting anywhere.
- p.3, §3.1: "the products consist of spherical particles, with about 16 nm in mean size"
  (Fig. 4(b)); shell thickness ~2 nm, "nearly independent of the NPs' size" (Fig. 5).
- p.4, §3.2: Sn4+ content 0.01–0.5 M changes shape/size dispersivity; at 0.01–0.1 M the NPs are
  "nearly unchanged in NPs' mean size and shell thickness (approximately 2 nm)".
- p.4: "the amount of Au@SnO2 NPs in the solution increases with duration of the laser ablation
  ... but the thickness of the shell was almost unchanged" — amount and shell thickness, not
  particle size, and duration is not fluence/energy/power/intensity.
- SI pp.1–4: film preparation, gas-sensing test, Fig. S1 (Tyndall/photos), Fig. S2 (Raman).
  No laser parameter, no size value, no fluence/energy/power/intensity word.

## Verdict

No claim. The laser is fixed; the varied variables are Sn4+ concentration (chemical) and
ablation duration (not one of the four laser variables). No sentence states that Au (or Au@SnO2)
particle size changes or does not change with fluence, energy, power or intensity.

## Rejected sentences and reasons

- p.1 abstract: "The spherical Au NPs (<20 nm in mean size) wrapped with a SnO2 layer of
  approximately 2 nm in thickness are formed after the laser ablation of a gold target in SnCl4
  solutions with concentrations of 0.01–0.1 M." — single size range at one laser setting; the
  varied quantity is SnCl4 concentration.
- p.1 abstract: "The thickness of such SnO2 shell is nearly independent of Au particle sizes." —
  shell thickness vs particle size, not size vs a laser variable.
- p.3: "The shell layers are approximately 2 nm in thickness for all NPs regardless of size,
  indicating that the shell thickness is nearly independent of the NPs' size." — same, shell
  thickness vs core size.
- p.4: "the amount of Au@SnO2 NPs in the solution increases with duration of the laser ablation,
  as expected, but the thickness of the shell was almost unchanged" — amount and shell thickness,
  not particle size; duration is not fluence/energy/power/intensity.
- p.4: "If the Sn 4+ content is too low, most of the Au NPs could not be wrapped with SnO2 shell
  due to insufficient tin source in the solution." — wrapping, not size; chemical variable.
- p.4: "the Sn4+ content in the solution influenced the quality of Au@SnO2 NPs, including the
  shape and size dispersivity" — chemical variable, and "quality/dispersivity", no laser variable.
- p.5–6, §3.3: plasma formation, electric double layer, electrostatic self-assembly, viscosity
  mechanism sentences — mechanism, no stated size-vs-laser dependence.
- p.6: "the method based on the laser ablation in water could produce such clean Au NPs" —
  surface cleanliness, no size dependence.
- p.7 conclusions: "the shell thickness is nearly independent of the particles' size" — shell
  thickness vs size, not a laser variable.
- p.6–7: SERS intensity and gas-sensing response sentences — performance, not size; "intensity"
  there is Raman intensity, not laser intensity.
- Fig. 4(b), Fig. 5, Fig. 6 captions — captions alone are excluded by claims_spec §2.

## Questions for the curator

None open. The article is a single-condition synthesis study; the only size statement tied to a
varied parameter concerns Sn4+ concentration, which is outside the four laser variables.
