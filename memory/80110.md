## Recorded claim

`80110-c1`, p.3, own_data, Au, `colloid`, `energy`, `affects`:
"One can see that the nanoparticle had a spherical shape, while variation of laser
pulse energy leads to a change of mean nanoparticle size ."

- The sentence sits in the results section next to Fig. 1 (own HR-TEM size
  distributions of Au NPs made by fs fragmentation of their own colloids), so the
  basis is `own_data`, not `cited`.
- `variable=energy`: the text says "laser pulse energy" and the only quantities it
  gives for this step are in uJ (Fig. 1 caption, p.4: 10 and 95 uJ). No J/cm2.
- `direction=affects`: "leads to a change" states a dependence without a sign.
- `medium_class=colloid`: the fragmentation step is performed on "preliminarily
  prepared Au colloids in water" (p.3), i.e. the synthesis medium contains a
  feedstock colloid, which is the first applicable class in results_spec §2. The
  liquid itself is deionized water (p.3, methods).

## Rejected sentences and reasons

- p.2 abstract: "By varying laser parameters (laser energy, focusing conditions)
  and environment (deionize d water, acetone), we were able to synthesize spherical
  Au and TiN nanoparticles of variable size between a few of nm and 30-40 nm under
  a relatively low size dispersion." — names focusing conditions next to laser
  energy, and the article does vary focusing (lens-target distance, p.3), so the
  "only fluence/energy/power/intensity varies" condition of claims_spec §2 is not
  met; it is also an abstract restatement of the p.3 dependence.
- p.3: "The energy dependence of mean NPs size was studied in detail in our
  previous study19." — cited restatement of the same dependence in the same
  results paragraph; recording it separately would count one dependence twice in
  prevalence (same rule as 534494).
- p.6 conclusions: "We found the possibility to tune NPs size by changing laser
  pulse energy, while implementation of additional fs laser fragmentation step
  resulted in reduction of mean NPs size down to 3-4 nm and significant narrowing
  of size distribution ." — conclusion restatement of the same dependence (and it
  covers TiN as well); one claim per dependence.
- p.4: "Our tests also showed that the mean size of formed TiN NPs depended on
  laser pulse energy similarly to the case of Au NPs16,17." — the stated subject is
  TiN (excluded material); Au appears only as a cited comparison.
- p.4: "In particular, the increase of pulse energy from 10 to 100 μJ led to the
  increase of NPs mean diameter from 25 to 40 nm under the size dispersion of
  25-40 nm FWHM." — TiN in acetone, excluded material.
- p.4: "For ablation in water, the dependence of NPs size on pulse energy was not
  significant and the mean size of synthesized TiN NPs was always around 30 nm
  under the size dispersion of 40 nm FWHM." — TiN, excluded material (a no_effect
  for TiN, not for Au).
- p.3: "Saturation concentration and time until saturation depended on pulse
  energy." — concentration/kinetics, not nanoparticle size.
- p.3: "the change of pulse energy from 10 uJ to 100 uJ led to a shift of lens
  position by 10 mm" — focal-position adjustment, no size stated.
- p.2: "While conventional nanosecond laser ablation often leads to large, widely
  size-dispersed nanoparticles9-15, the employment of ultra-short (femtosecond)
  laser ablation and fragmentation can lead to the fabrication of extremely stable
  colloidal solutions of low-size-dispersed bare (ligand-free) NPs16-23." — the
  named laser property is pulse duration, not fluence/energy/power/intensity.
- p.5: extinction-peak positions for 5 nm vs 30 nm Au and TiN NPs — optical
  properties, not a size-vs-laser-variable dependence.
- Fig. 1 and Fig. 2 captions — captions alone are not claims (claims_spec §2).
