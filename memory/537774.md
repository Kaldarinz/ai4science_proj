# 537774 — Letzel et al., Appl. Surf. Sci. 435 (2018) 743-751

"Primary particle diameter differentiation and bimodality identification by five analytical
methods using gold nanoparticle size distributions synthesized by pulsed laser ablation in
liquids". Main 9 pp, supplied SI 10 pp (figures only).

## Claim recorded

- 537774-c1, p.3, basis `cited`: "the minimum particle diameter accessible by means of LFL of
  AuNP is a function of the applied laser fluence [23]". Direction `affects` (dependence stated,
  no direction). `variable=fluence` (mJ/cm2 in the same sentence). `material=Au` (AuNP).
  `medium_class=colloid`: the sentence concerns LFL, i.e. irradiation of an existing AuNP
  colloid; the article's own LFL runs on aqueous Au colloids (p.3). The sentence itself names
  no liquid, so `colloid` rests on the LFL operation, not on a stated medium — flagged here for
  the curator.

## Rejected sentences and reasons

- p.3 own LFL conditions ("irradiated with 25 mJ/cm2 for 5 min and 108 mJ/cm2 for 10 min"):
  two different fluences are applied, but to two different starting colloids (S7, S10) and for
  two different durations, and the text states no size change with fluence. A dependence the
  reader would have to infer by comparing reported sizes is data, not a claim (claims_spec §2).
- p.2 "in recent work, we performed SAXS during the ablation of gold in a micromolar solution of
  sodium chloride [28], which is known to stabilize the resulting gold nanoparticles and
  decrease their size due to additional surface charges [29]": the varied factor is the NaCl
  additive, not a laser variable.
- p.2 "primary particle size shifts from 10 nm to 7 nm cannot be statistically and significantly
  resolved by in situ SAXS ... but can clearly be differentiated ex situ": statement about
  method resolution, not about a laser variable.
- p.2 "the minimum particle diameter accessible by means of LFL ... is a function of the applied
  laser fluence" restated nowhere else; the abstract, results and conclusions contain no
  fluence-size statement, so no duplicate claim.
- p.7 "analyzing trends of primary particle diameter shifts during colloid laser-synthesis is
  important to understand, e.g. size quenching mechanisms": motivation, no dependence stated.
- p.2 "as the particles still grow inside the bubble the results might not be directly
  comparable": in-situ vs ex-situ comparability, no laser variable.
- p.3 "the large area of the raw beam (6 mm2) ensures to treat all particles with the same
  average pulse energy": states a constant pulse energy, no size dependence.
- Table 1/2 values and Figures 1-4, S1-S13 captions: data and axis labels, not claims.
- Reference titles containing "fluence"/"influence" (e.g. ref. 35): reference list excluded.

## Keyword sweep (both PDFs)

"fluence" occurs once as a physical quantity (p.3 claim sentence) plus "influence" (pp.7, 8 and
reference titles). "power" only as the laser's 7 W (p.2), "powerful" (p.8) and reference titles.
"intensity" only as scattering/light intensity (pp.1, 2, 5). "energy" only as the constant
average pulse energy (p.3), the 11.2 keV X-ray photon energy (p.3) and a reference title.
"mJ/cm2" only in the two own LFL values (p.3). No J/cm2, W/cm2, uJ or mJ series exists.
