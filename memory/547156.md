## Verdict

One retained claim, `547156-c1`, basis `cited`; no own-data claim.
Former claim `547156-c2` was removed by curator Anton on 2026-11-17 (see below).

- `547156-c1`, p.2, `energy`, `increase`, `cited`, Au, `water`:
  "We note that the relative contribution of the plasma-related mechanism can be weakened or undetected
  by a decrease of laser energy during the experiment,41 leading to a reduction of the mean size of the
  nanoparticles produced in deionized water."
  The sentence states the direction explicitly: a decrease of laser energy gives a reduction of the mean
  size, i.e. size increases with energy. Unit is mJ/pulse (p.2), so `variable=energy` by claims_spec §5.
  Medium is named in the sentence ("deionized water") → `water`. Basis `cited`: the dependence is
  attributed to ref. 41 (Kabashin & Meunier, J. Appl. Phys. 2003, 94, 7941), the authors' previous study,
  not to this article's experiments.

## Curator decision — 2026-11-17

Curator: Anton. Decision: remove `547156-c2` from the extracted claims.

Rejected sentence, `10.1021_jp047134.pdf`, PDF p.5, Discussion:
> A femtosecond laser, with higher intensity and lower energy, forms smaller nanoparticles than does a nanosecond laser.39-41

Rationale supplied by the curator: the comparison conflates pulse-duration and pulse-energy effects.
The sentence compares femtosecond and nanosecond lasers while intensity and pulse energy also differ;
it does not isolate a size dependence on a single energy/intensity variable. Do not reintroduce this
sentence as an energy or intensity claim. The separate cited energy-increase claim `547156-c1` on p.2
is retained. This resolves the former curator question about c2; no general specification change or
approval of other extraction/matching decisions is implied.

## Why the article has no own-data claim

All of the article's own experiments run at one laser energy: "The laser energy was fixed at 0.2 mJ/pulse
during all experiments unless otherwise specified" (p.2), and Table 1 lists 0.2 mJ/pulse for deionized
water, 10 mM NaCl, 10 mM KCl and NaOH pH 9.4, and 0.25 mJ/pulse for 10 mM propylamine. The varied factor
is the aqueous medium (salts, pH, amine), not fluence, energy, power or intensity. The size differences
between media are therefore medium effects, not laser-variable claims.

## Rejected sentences and reasons

- p.2 "Typically, the focal plane was adjusted 1.5 mm beyond the target surface to decrease the radiation
  intensity on its surface and enlarge the radiation spot." and "This weakened the plasma intensity and
  thus minimized the impact of plasma-related ablation effects that were found to broaden the size
  distribution of the ablated nanoparticles.41" — the dependent quantity is the width of the size
  distribution, not the size; the intensity change is produced by defocusing (spot enlargement), not by a
  controlled fluence variation; mechanism explanation with a citation.
- p.2 "In this study, the energy we used, 0.2 mJ/pulse, was a compromise between the relatively small
  nanoparticle size and the relatively high production efficiency." — a rationale for the chosen setting,
  not a statement that size changes with energy; no direction is asserted.
- p.2 "Under our conditions, the maximum of the first radiation-related distribution was around 20 nm,
  whereas the maximum of the broad, plasma-related one was around 70 nm." — reported values (data), no
  laser-variable dependence stated.
- p.2 "Basically, 3-30 nm nanoparticles are known to show the plasmon-related peak around 520 nm." —
  general knowledge about the plasmon peak, not a size-versus-laser-variable statement.
- p.4 "It should be noted that this is no longer the case when a very high laser fluence is used." — the
  closest fluence sentence, but "this" refers to the single-Gaussian fit / salt-controlled growth, not to
  the particle size; no size change is stated. Recorded here as the nearest miss.
- p.4 "Ablation (250 mJ/pulse) in 10 mM n-propylamine resulted in bright red gold sols after several
  minutes, indicating the formation of small gold particles." — one energy value, no dependence; the
  energy is also inconsistent with Table 1 (0.25 mJ/pulse), see textual notes.
- p.5 "Compared to those produced by ablation in deionized water, the gold particles produced in NaCl and
  KCl were smaller for identical laser fluences." — medium effect at identical fluence; the sentence
  explicitly holds the laser variable fixed.
- p.5 "The absolute surface charge of the gold nanoparticles, which increases with pH, may have a
  determining role on the size control of the particles produced and may explain the reason nanoparticles
  prepared in NaOH solutions were smaller than those produced in deionized water." — pH/medium effect.
- p.5 "Ablation in n-propylamine also results in nanoparticles smaller than those obtained in deionized
  water." — medium effect.
- p.6 Conclusions "The addition of Cl- or OH- to the solution increases the absolute value of this
  negative charge and leads to the formation of smaller nanoparticles." — medium effect.
- p.1 abstract "This limited the coalescence of the particles, due to electrostatic repulsion, and led to
  a significant reduction of their size." — medium/surface-chemistry effect.
- All figure captions and axis labels; the reference list.
