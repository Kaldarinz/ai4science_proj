## Verdict: two own-data claims (both p.3, results section)
- `80094-c1` — Au in deionized water, `increase`: "Indeed, the mean size of nanoparticles increased from
  8-10 to 30 nm under the increase of laser energy from 50 to 175 µJ/pulse".
- `80094-c2` — AuSi in aqueous Si-nanoparticle solution, `no_effect`: "In contrast, the increase of laser
  energy did not affect the size of nanoparticles under the ablation in solutions of Si nanoparticles, as the
  mean size and size dispersion were always around 5-8 nm and 3-5 nm FWHM, respectively (Fig. 1c,d)."
- Variable `energy` for both: the text says "laser energy"/"laser fluence", but the varied quantity is given
  in µJ/pulse (Fig. 1c,d and Fig. 3 axis labels "Laser fluence (μJ/pulse)"; range 50–175 µJ/pulse), so
  claims_spec §5 maps it to `energy`, not `fluence`. `variable_written` keeps the authors' words.
- Medium: c1 `water` (pure deionized water, no additives); c2 `colloid` (ablation performed in an aqueous
  solution of preliminarily prepared Si nanoparticles, i.e. a feedstock colloid is present).
- Material: c1 `Au` (bare Au NPs), c2 `AuSi` (hybrid Au-Si NPs; the article's own label "AuSi NPs").
- Conditions: fs pulses (Ti:sapphire, 800 nm, 110 fs, 1 kHz).

## Rejected sentences and why
- p.3 "Dependence s of the average size of nanoparticles a nd their size dispersion (full width at half
  maximum, FWHM) on applied laser fluence is shown in Fig. 1c and Fig. 1d, respectively." — figure pointer;
  states no size change and no direction. The dependence itself is stated in the two following sentences.
- p.3 "Here, one can mention a strong laser energy dependence of size for pure Au NPs." — lead-in to `c1`
  in the same paragraph; the direction is given by the next sentence, so it is not recorded as a separate
  `affects` claim (one claim per material/medium/variable/direction/basis; the shortest run stating the
  claim is the `c1` sentence).
- p.4 "Furthermore, the independence of size of AuSi NPs of applied laser fluence indicates a much stronger
  interaction between gold and silicon species than that of between purely gold nanoclusters." — restatement
  of `c2` (same material/medium/variable/direction/basis), later occurrence; quote kept from Results.
- p.4 "It is interesting that the increase of laser fluence does not affect the chemical composition a nd
  size of AuSi NPs." — restatement of `c2`; also mixes composition, which is not a size claim.
- p.4 summary "The mean size, size dispersion and composition of Au-Si nanohybrids are independent of
  pumping laser energy." — restatement of `c2` in the conclusions; not duplicated.
- p.4 "The increase of the laser fluence can much increase the number of ejected nanoclusters and their
  energy. When ablated in deionized water, gold nanoclusters do not have any limiting mechanisms and can
  grow to several tens and even hundreds of nm (Fig. 1c,d)." — mechanism explanation; the first sentence
  concerns the number and energy of nanoclusters, not particle size, and the second states no fluence
  dependence (claims_spec §2 excludes mechanism explanations that state no dependence).
- p.1 abstract "the stoichiometry of colloids did not depend on laser fluence during the fabrication
  experiments" — chemical composition, not size.
- p.2 Fig. 1 caption "Dependence of average size (c) and size dispersion (d) of Au NPs and AuSi NPs on laser
  energy." — caption alone, excluded by claims_spec §2.
- p.3 "One can see that no dependence of Au/Si ratio of AuSi NPs on used laser fluence was obtained." and
  "the increase of laser energy did not change the proportion of Si in the nanoparticle composition" —
  composition (Au/Si ratio), not size.
- p.3 "the mean size of nanoparticles was much smaller (5-8 nm)" and p.4 "the mean size of formed Au-Si
  nanohybrids was much sm aller than pure Au nanoparticles (Fig. 1)" — medium comparison (water vs Si
  colloid), not a laser-variable dependence.
- p.2 "with the mean size ranging from a few nm to hundreds of nm" — single-condition size range, no
  laser variable.
