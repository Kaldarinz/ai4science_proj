## Verdict: one own-data claim, Au, medium `water`, variable `fluence`
- `538022-c1` — p.4, section 3 results (TEM): "The average diameter was observed to be decreased to 5-10 nm and
  the size distribution to be narrowed as the laser fluence was increased up to 160 J/cm2." Direction `decrease`,
  basis `own_data`, material Au, medium_class `water` (double-distilled water, "in the absence of any additives",
  p.1/p.2), conditions "ns pulses (10 ns), 10 Hz, 532 nm; ablation fluence 10-250 J/cm2; TEM comparison
  95 -> 160 J/cm2".
- The same dependence is stated earlier from the spectra, p.3: "As a rule at higher fluences the plasmon peak was
  slightly blue shifted suggesting a certain decreasing of the particle size." Same (material, medium, variable,
  direction, basis), so one claim (claims_spec §2); the TEM sentence is quoted because it is the direct size
  measurement, the spectral sentence is an inference from the plasmon position.
- The generic framings are the same dependence restated without direction and are not separate claims: p.1
  abstract "both the mean size of the nanoparticles and their stability could be varied by proper selection of the
  parameters of laser ablation and postirradiation such as laser fluence and wavelength combinations"; p.4 "The
  size and size distribution of the nanoparticles were found to be functions of the laser ablation parameters
  (laser wavelength and fluence)"; p.5 conclusion "The resultant nanoparticles morphology was found to be
  determined by the processes of the laser-induced fragmentation occurring concurrently with a particle growth
  which were dependent on laser fluence, laser wavelength, and postablation irradiation".

  property, not about nanoparticle size (claims_spec §2).
- p.2 "The height and the width of the SPR peaks were found to be dependent upon the laser fluence." — optical
  property, not size.
- p.3 "The density of the ablated species can be changed by adjusting the laser fluence." and p.3 "By controlling
  the density of the ablated species it is possible to control the final size of the nanoparticles." — mechanism

  that state no dependence).
- p.3 "Therefore, the obtained spectra of gold colloids suggest increasing both the total abundance of gold atoms
  in the solution and the concentration of nanoparticles having diameters in the nanometer range as the laser

- p.3 "But for all that the total abundance of gold increased almost linearly with increasing laser fluence, and

## Questions for the curator
1. The article states the same fluence dependence twice with different strength: a directional statement (p.4 TEM
   "decreased ... as the laser fluence was increased up to 160 J/cm2", p.3 spectra "suggesting a certain decreasing
   of the particle size") and a directionless one (p.4 "functions of the laser ablation parameters (laser
   wavelength and fluence)", p.5 conclusion, p.1 abstract). I recorded one claim with direction `decrease` and
   treated the directionless sentences as restatements (claims_spec §2). If the curator reads the directionless
   framing as a distinct claim, the article gains a second own-data claim with direction `affects`, which would
   also need its own candidate audit.
2. The p.4 "functions of the laser ablation parameters (laser wavelength and fluence)" sentence attributes the
   dependence to wavelength and fluence jointly, and the article varies both (532 vs 266 nm, p.5: 266 nm gives the
   smallest size). The recorded claim is restricted to fluence; the wavelength part is not a claim under
   claims_spec §5 and is not recorded.
3. The p.3 spectral sentence is an inference from the plasmon peak position rather than a TEM measurement. It is
   the same claim as the TEM sentence, so nothing depends on it, but if the curator prefers the first occurrence
   as the quote, the claim content is unchanged.
