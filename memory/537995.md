## Verdict: two own-data claims, both Au, medium `water`, variable `fluence`
- `537995-c1` — p.4, section 3.2 (Au, fundamental wavelength): "Particles created by the lower value of the laser
  fluence show a low stage of agglomeration. This process is better expressed at the higher laser fluence, which is
  seen in the micrograph in Fig. 4 b. The mean particle size in both cases is approximately the same." Direction
  `no_effect`, basis `own_data`, material Au, medium_class `water` (double distilled water, "without any
  additives", p.5), conditions "ns pulses (15 ns), 10 Hz, 1064 nm, 5.8 and 15.5 J/cm2".
  The run of three sentences is the shortest that grounds "both cases": the claim sentence alone does not name the
  laser variable, and the two preceding sentences establish the lower and the higher fluence of Fig. 4a/b. The
  Fig. 4 caption gives the same two fluences (5.8 and 15.5 J/cm2) for Au at 1064 nm.
- `537995-c2` — p.5, conclusions: "The average particle size remains practically independent of the laser fluence
  at the fundamental wavelength, but its strong increase with the increasing of the laser fluence at the SHG for
  both materials has been established." Direction `increase`, basis `own_data`, material Au (the sentence says
  "both materials"; the Ag part is excluded by claims_spec §2), medium_class `water`, conditions "ns pulses
  (15 ns), 10 Hz, 532 nm (SHG), 5.3 and 19.9 J/cm2".
  The results-section statement of the same dependence (p.4 "At the higher laser fluence the micrograph shows
  larger separated sphericallike shaped particles and much Au material distributed as relatively narrow arrays")
  describes the micrograph appearance and the authors themselves call the fluence comparison "very provisional"
  (p.5), so the explicit size statement is quoted from the conclusions. The abstract's "Dependence of the mean
  particle size at the SHG on the laser fluence was established" is the same dependence restated (claims_spec §2:
  one claim, quote the results section if it states it, otherwise the first occurrence).
- The two claims are distinct by direction (`no_effect` at 1064 nm, `increase` at SHG), so they are two claims,
  not a restatement of one.
