# 80134 — Al-Kattan et al., Int. J. Mol. Sci. 2018, 19, 1563 (review)

Review of laser-ablative synthesis of bare Au and Si NPs and their use as
scaffold additives. One main PDF (15 pp), no supplied SI.

## Evidence locations

- p.4 §2: the only two body occurrences of "fluence", both in general
  statements about PLAL, not about a measured size dependence.
- p.5 §3.1: Si synthesis protocol (Yb:KGW, 1025 nm, 480 fs, 1 kHz, 1 h);
  "tunable mean side between 10 and 100 nm" — Si, no laser variable named.
- p.6: Si size control by initial microparticle concentration (not laser).
- p.8 §3.2: Au synthesis protocol (Yb:KGW, 1025 nm, 480 fs, 1 kHz, 15 min,
  7 ml deionized water, 99.99% target) — single condition, no fluence or
  pulse energy given; Fig. 6 HR-TEM + size distribution, one distribution.
- p.9 §4: scaffold results (fiber diameter, thermal stability), not NP size.
- p.10 §5 conclusions: "structural properties ... can be easily designed to
  control their size and dissolution behavior" — no laser variable.
- p.7: "intensity of 2 W/cm2" is RF hyperthermia irradiation, not laser.

## Text-layer artefact

Pages 3-4 (and 5-9) carry a duplicated second text layer of the accepted
manuscript ("int. j. mol. sci. 2018, 19, x") with different spacing. Both
copies normalize to the same strings; quotes were taken from the published
layer and verified in the normalized page text.

## Rejected sentences and reasons

1. p.4 "furthermore, all processes take place simultaneously during a very
   short time period (a few ns) and depend on laser parameters (pulses
   length, wavelength, fluence, etc.) [75]." — mechanism statement about the
   ablation process, no size dependence, cited (ref. 75).
2. p.4 "in general, plal looks as a reliable tool ... control nps
   characteristics by adjusting laser parameters (focusing point position,
   fluence, repetition rate, fragmentation duration, etc.) and
   physicochemical conditions ..." — recorded as the no-claim quote: several
   laser parameters named at once, "characteristics" not size, no direction.
3. p.4 "due its specific conditions, much less radiation is transferred to
   the cavitation bubble, which limits cavitation phenomena and thus makes
   possible much more rigorous control of nps size and size dispersion
   [50,75]." — the named laser property is the ultrashort (fs) regime, i.e.
   pulse duration, not fluence/energy/power/intensity; cited.
4. p.2 "here, the employment of ultra-short laser pulses can lead to the
   fabrication of extremely stable colloidal solutions of low-size-dispersed
   crystalline nps even in the absence of any protective ligands [62,63,65]."
   — pulse duration, cited, no direction.
5. p.5 "physicochemical characterization showed that so formed si nps have a
   tunable mean side between 10 and 100 nm under narrow size dispersion." —
   Si (other material), and no laser variable is named.
6. p.6 "other advantages of plal approach are related to the possibility of
   controlling mean nps size by varying the initial si microparticles
   concentration [66]." — Si, and the varied quantity is concentration.
7. p.9 "other parameter such as the size of nps has to be varied to highlight
   their effect on the physicochemical properties of the fibers." — NP size
   is the independent variable, not the laser.
8. p.10 "second, structural properties of laser-synthesized nps can be easily
   designed to control their size and dissolution behavior." — no laser
   variable, no direction.
9. Fig. 5 caption (p.7) RF intensity 2 W/cm2 — hyperthermia treatment
   parameter, not a laser synthesis variable.

## Decisions

- No claim: the review varies no laser parameter and states no size-versus-
  fluence/energy/power/intensity dependence for Au (or Au-containing) NPs.
- One no_claims row, quote from p.4 (the closest sentence).
- Reading attestation `complete`, bound to the single main-PDF hash.

## Questions for the curator

- None blocking. If the curator considers "control nps characteristics by

  one `general`/`unspecified` claim; the sentence is recorded verbatim in the
