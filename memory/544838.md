## Verdict

One own-data claim, `544838-c1` (p.9): the size of the gold particles produced by irradiating the AuNPs(II)
nanochain colloid grows with pulse energy up to the fragmentation threshold and decreases above it —
`direction=non_monotonic`, `variable=energy`, `material=Au`, `medium_class=colloid`.

## Variable decision

`energy`, not `fluence`. The varied quantity is the pulse energy, stated in µJ/mJ (4 µJ → 1.5 mJ) in §3.3 and
in the figure caption; the energy density is given only as a derived equivalent ("and, accordingly, an energy
density of 12 mJ/cm2 to 4.9 J/cm2", p.9). claims_spec §5 decides by the unit of the quantity the text varies,
so the µJ/mJ scale gives `energy`. The abstract and p.9 also call the threshold "threshold energy (321 mJ/cm2)",
i.e. a fluence unit for a quantity named "energy"; that is the same threshold expressed per area and does not
change the varied quantity. Curator question: if the project prefers the per-area threshold as the primary
variable, the claim would be `fluence`; the text supports both readings, and the two scales are proportional
here (fixed 200 µm spot).

## Claim set decision (why one claim, not two or three)

The article states one dependence of particle size on pulse energy with a maximum at the fragmentation
threshold. It appears as (a) one abstract sentence (p.1) with both branches, (b) two consecutive branch
statements in the results (p.9), and (c) restatements in the discussion (p.14: "Varying the laser pulse energy
also allows for the control of the size of the resulting particles. The highest efficiency is achieved using
pulses with an energy density slightly below the threshold energy density for laser fragmentation") and in the
conclusions (p.14: size "is influenced by … the energy and duration of the laser pulse"). claims_spec §2 makes a
dependence restated in abstract, results and conclusions one claim, quoted from the results, so the three
sentences of p.9 are quoted once and the restatements are folded in.

Alternative considered and rejected: recording the two branches as separate `increase` and `decrease` claims
(the pattern of 538366-c1/c2/c3). Rejected because here the two branches are one dependence with one threshold,
stated consecutively in a single run of sentences; splitting them would record two monotone claims for a
non-monotonic dependence and would double-count the same assertion. Curator question: if the project prefers
branch claims, `544838-c2` (`increase`, 4–57 µJ) and `544838-c3` (`decrease`, 76 µJ–1.5 mJ) would be added from
the same p.9 sentences.

## Medium decision

`colloid`. Stage III irradiates 2 mL of the AuNPs(II) colloid — gold nanochains dispersed in deionized water,
prepared in stage II and fed into the cuvette as the starting material ("AuNPs(III) samples were obtained by
repeated irradiation of colloids of elongated AuNPs(II) gold nanochains", p.4). results_spec §2 gives `colloid`
first for a medium that contains a feedstock colloid, ahead of `water` (one water constituent, no additives).
No additives, ligands or surfactants are used anywhere in the synthesis (the reagent-free claim is the point of
the paper).

## Rejected sentences and reasons

- p.1 abstract: "Increasing the concentration of nanoparticles in the initial colloid up to 150 µg/mL leads to a
  linear increase in the size of submicron nanoparticles." — concentration, not a laser variable.
- p.1 abstract: "The use of picosecond pulses for irradiating nanochains demonstrates the formation of the largest
  particles (200 nm) compared to nanosecond pulses" — pulse duration, not fluence/energy/power/intensity.
- p.1 abstract: "The formation of submicron gold particles upon irradiation of 2 mL of colloid occurs within the
  first minutes of irradiation and is complete after 480,000 laser pulses." — irradiation time / pulse number.
- p.2: "The resulting size of the smaller particles can be controlled by varying the radiation parameters [17,18]."
  — "radiation parameters" is not restricted to fluence/energy/power/intensity (the article itself varies
  wavelength, duration, time and concentration), so §2's unnamed-parameter rule does not apply; also a general
  statement about fragmentation, no size direction.
- p.2: "Currently, one of the main strategies for increasing the production of nanoparticles is the optimization of
  laser radiation parameters (laser fluence, wavelength, repetition rate, pulse duration) during the synthesis
  process [33,34]" — productivity, not size; four parameters named.
- p.7: "A shift of the absorption maximum toward longer wavelengths indicates an increase in the size of the
  particles in the colloid" — spectral inference at one setting, no varied laser variable.
- p.8: "It was shown that with increasing colloid irradiation time, the amplitude of the plasmon resonance peak in
  the absorption spectra increases." — irradiation time, not a laser variable of the four.
- p.9: "In [20], it was shown that the threshold values of laser radiation fluence for the onset of the laser
  fragmentation process … are in the range of several hundred mJ/cm 2." — cited statement of a fragmentation-onset
  threshold, not a statement that size changes with fluence; the size-change statement is the article's own (c1).
- p.9: "It is shown that an increase in the pulse energy to 0.1 mJ (321 mJ/cm2) leads to a monotonic change in the
  absorption maximum position from 525 nm to 539 nm." — SPR peak position, not particle size; the size statement is
  in the preceding run.
- p.10: "the use of picosecond laser pulses allows the synthesis of larger submicron gold particles compared to
  nanosecond pulses" — pulse duration.
- p.12: "an increase in the concentration of particles in the irradiated colloid from 25 µg/mL to 150 µg/mL leads to
  an increase in the amplitude of the plasmon resonance peak and its shift towards longer wavelengths" — concentration.
- p.13: "It is important to note that the laser pulse energy does not exceed the evaporation energy of the nanochain
  material. Otherwise, a decrease in particle size would be observed due to laser fragmentation." — mechanism
  statement about the chosen regime; the same threshold dependence as c1, not a separate claim.
- p.13: "Since the rate of nanoparticle aggregation directly depends on the concentration, with increasing
  concentration in the colloid, there will be longer chains … This, when they are irradiated, will lead to an
  increase in the size of the nanoparticles." — concentration.
- p.14: "We have shown the existence of optimal parameters of laser radiation (… pulse energy/fluence up to threshold
  values of laser fragmentation of 100–300 mJ/cm2 …)" — restatement of c1.
- All figure captions (Figures 1–9) and axis labels — excluded by §2.

## Textual notes for the curator

- The energy series is reported mainly through the SPR maximum position (Figure 6B), not through a tabulated size
  per energy; the only size statements for the energy series are the qualitative "larger than in the original
  AuNPs(I) colloid" and "a decrease in the size of the nanoparticles".
- The threshold is given as 321 mJ/cm2 (0.1 mJ) on p.9 and as "100–300 mJ/cm2" on p.14; the abstract repeats
  321 mJ/cm2.
- The 200 ns experiments use 30 s instead of 8 min (p.10), so the pulse-duration comparison is not at equal pulse
  number; this concerns a non-claim variable and is noted only for completeness.
- Extracted text renders "4µJ" and "57µJ" without a space (pypdf), and hyphenates "nanopar-ticles" across a line
  break on p.9; the quote follows the extracted text with the hyphenation joined, as claims_spec §4 normalization
  allows.
