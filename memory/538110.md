## Verdict: no claim

The article varies no fluence, energy, power or intensity to study size. Ablation is one fixed
setting (1025 nm, 480 fs, 500 uJ, 1-5 kHz, p.2) and fragmentation one stated fluence
(about 1 J/cm2, p.2). The reported size differences follow the fabrication modality (ablation
15 +/- 10 nm vs 40 min fragmentation 24 +/- 5 nm) and the fragmentation duration (40 min vs
20 min -> 15-17 nm), neither of which is a laser-variable dependence under claims_spec
section 2.

Closest sentence, used as the no-claim quote (p.6): "it is possible to control to some extent
the width of the size distribution of nanoparticles by choice of focusing, propagation length
inside water, and pulse energy, which define supercontinuum generation." It fails section 2 on
three counts: it concerns the width of the size distribution, not the nanoparticle size; it
names focusing and propagation length next to pulse energy, so the "of the laser parameters,
only fluence/energy/power/intensity varied" condition is not met; and it is attributed to
ref. 44 (Kabashin, Laser Phys. 2009).

## Rejected sentences and reasons

- p.2 "the employment of femtosecond laser radiation is of particular interest for nanoparticle
  synthesis, as it cannot only provide superior and controllable size characteristics,29,30 but
  can also improve solution stability ..." — the named laser property is pulse duration, not
  fluence/energy/power/intensity; cited (refs 29, 30); no direction.
- p.2 "although the size distribution of gold nanoparticles prepared by conventional "long"
  (nanosecond) laser ablation in deionized water is typically very broadened,21-28 it can be
  much improved by adding reactive surfactants (eg, sodium dodecyl sulfate)." — cited; the
  varied factor is the surfactant, and the property is distribution width.
- p.2 "we used relatively low laser fluences (1 j . cm2) to avoid the phenomenon of
  laser-assisted plasma breakdown of the liquid, but the radiation intensity was high enough to
  ablate the suspended nanoparticles." — states the fixed fluence and a breakdown-avoidance
  rationale; no size statement.
- p.2 "from statistical size analysis, the mean size and size dispersion were 15 +/- 10 nm at
  full width half maximum (fwhm), as shown in figure 1." — a single size value at one fixed
  setting; a value, not a dependence (claims_spec section 2).
- p.3 "as shown in figure 2, the mean size of nanoparticles was about 24 nm, while the size
  dispersion was very weak (less than 10 nm fwhm)." and "we carried out fragmentation for 20
  minutes, which led to a smaller mean size (15-17 nm) and broader size dispersion (14 nm
  fwhm)." — the varied quantity is the fragmentation duration, not fluence/energy/power/
  intensity; the fluence of fragmentation is not stated to differ between the two runs.
- p.4 "by controlling femtosecond-pulse energy in ablation and fragmentation conditions,
  fabrication of colloidal nanoparticles was optimized for solution stability." — names pulse
  energy but states that fabrication was optimized for solution stability, not that size
  changes with energy; no size value or direction is attached to the energy control.
- p.4 "it was found that the most stable solution (months without sedimentation) was obtained
  at the most narrow size distribution of preferentially spherical au nanoparticles." — links
  stability to distribution width, not size to a laser variable.
- p.5 "sample number 2a had a narrower size distribution of the colloidal particles, while
  sample number 1 had more size poly-dispersed colloidal nanoparticles." — compares two
  fabrication protocols (ablation vs partial fragmentation), not two laser-variable settings.
- p.6 "sample number 2a has spherical and rod-like particles of aspect ratio close to 2
  (figure 4b); sample number 1 is dominated by spherical particles which have a large size
  distribution d = 15 +/- 14 nm (figure 4a)." — single values per sample, no laser variable.
- p.10 conclusion "a facile method of fabricating stable gold nanoparticles of controlled size
  and shape distribution by femtosecond-laser ablation and fragmentation in water is
  demonstrated." — no laser variable named, no direction, and the article varies none of the
  four; a capability statement, not a dependence.
- p.10 "laser produced au nanoparticles can be centrifuged for even better size separation." —
  centrifugation, not a laser variable.
- Figure captions (Figs. 1-9) and axis labels — excluded by claims_spec section 2.

## Questions for the curator

1. The p.6 sentence is the only place where pulse energy is tied to a size-distribution
   property. If the curator reads "width of the size distribution" as within the scope of
   "nanoparticle size" and accepts a multi-parameter control statement as a claim, the article
   would gain one `cited` claim (variable `energy`, direction `affects`). I recorded no claim
   because section 2 requires a size statement and the single-varied-parameter condition.
2. The p.4 "by controlling femtosecond-pulse energy ... optimized for solution stability"
   sentence implies that pulse energy was varied during optimization, but no energy values and
   no size values per energy are given. If the curator treats it as an own-data dependence
   statement, the article would gain one `own_data` claim (variable `energy`, direction
   `affects`). I recorded no claim because no size change is stated.
