## Verdict: no claim (one `no_claims.csv` row)

The article varies no laser parameter. One fixed setting throughout (140 fs, 800 nm, 1 kHz,
500 uJ, fluence 0.20 J/cm2). Varied factors: initial colloid size/type, dextran additive, solution
volume, treatment duration (2 h). Under `claims_spec.md` §2 an unnamed "laser parameters" /
"radiation parameters" dependence is a claim only when, of the laser parameters, the article varies
only fluence, energy, power or intensity; here none is varied, so the abstract and p.2 sentences
are general statements about the method. Same reading as the 532516 no-claim precedent.

## Rejected sentences and reasons

- p.1 abstract "depends strongly on laser parameters" — no laser parameter varied; no direction;
  no size value at a second fluence/energy. Recorded as the no-claim quote.
- p.2 "depended on the radiation parameters and on the presence of chemical additives" — same
  defect; restatement of the abstract.
- p.2 "the final size of the nanoparticles is determined by the chemical interaction of the
  fragmented species in the solution" — mechanism explanation, no laser variable.
- p.2 "depending on the initial pulse energy and on the focusing condition, ~10% of the initial
  energy ... was transferred in the spectral band of 400-650 nm" — supercontinuum conversion
  efficiency, not nanoparticle size.
- p.3 "Link et al. clearly observed the melting of nanorods and their conversion to nanoparticles
  under the irradiation of a small solution volume by 0.56 J/cm2 femtosecond pulses, while even
  prolonged irradiation of nanoparticles did not change their size significantly." — cited; the
  varied factor is irradiation duration, not fluence; no size-versus-fluence statement.
- p.3 "We believe that a low efficiency of nanoparticle fragmentation in these tests, using much
  higher laser intensities ..., was explained by a much smaller solution volume" — cited; about
  fragmentation efficiency, explained by volume, not by intensity.
- p.2 "the presence of dextran led to a decrease of the size and size dispersion down to 6+-2 nm" —
  additive effect, not a laser variable.
- p.2 "Preliminary results on silicon and silver colloids also showed the same trend of size
  reduction under the irradiation of the white light based irradiation." — other materials, and
  irradiation duration, not a laser variable.
- All figure captions (Figs. 1-3) and axis labels; conclusions on p.3.
