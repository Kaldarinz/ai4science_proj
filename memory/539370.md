## Verdict: no claim

The article varies **no laser fluence, energy, power or intensity**. Section 2.1 (p.3) states one fixed
setting: 1064 nm, 5 ns, 10 Hz, 0.980 mW, 106 mJ, 86.4 cm lens. The two varied factors are the
irradiation time (5, 10, 15, 20 min) and the solvent pH (2.6, 4.8, 8.1, 10.3). Size is reported against
those two factors only: Table 1 (size vs irradiation time, p.6) and Table 2 (z-size vs pH, p.10).

Keyword sweep over the normalized pages (checker's pypdf extractor):
- "fluence" — 3 hits, all false positives: "influence" on p.10 and in reference titles on pp.13-14.
  No fluence value in J/cm2 or mJ/cm2 anywhere in the article.
- "energy" — 1 hit, the single 106 mJ setting on p.3. No other energy value.
- "power" — 2 hits: the single 0.980 mW ablation setting (p.3) and the 10-60 mW SERS excitation power
  at the samples (p.3), which is a Raman measurement parameter, not an ablation variable, and carries no
  size statement.
- "intensity" — 5 hits, all Raman/SERS signal intensity (pp.2, 5, 9) or "influence" (p.10, p.13).
- "irradiance" — absent. No W/cm2 or GW/cm2 value anywhere.

So the claims_spec §2 condition for an unnamed "laser parameters" claim (of the laser parameters, only
fluence/energy/power/intensity varied) is not met: nothing in that set is varied.

## Textual inconsistencies recorded for the curator

- p.3 "the laser was operated in single-shot mode (5 ns, 10 hz)" — single-shot and 10 Hz are mutually
  exclusive as stated.
- p.3 "the laser power used was 0.980 mw, and th e energy was 106 mj" — 106 mJ at 10 Hz is ~1.06 W, not
  0.98 mW; the two numbers differ by three orders of magnitude and cannot both describe the same beam.
- p.4 gives the large Au spheres as 126 ± 36 nm, the p.5 Figure 2 caption as 126 ± 39 nm.
- p.4 and the p.5 caption swap the Ag colours: the text calls the 132 ± 5 nm particles green-gray and the
  5 ± 1 nm seeds yellow, the caption calls the 132 ± 5 nm ones yellow and the 5 ± 1 nm ones green-gray.
- Table 1 (p.6) is non-monotonic for Au (96, 82, 71, 75 nm at 5, 10, 15, 20 min) while the text says the
  size "is reduced to 75 nm" as the time increases; the 15 min value (71 nm) is the minimum.
- Section 2.3 (p.3) states the pH values 2.6, 4.8, 8.1 and 10.3, but Table 2 (p.10) lists 4.2/4.3, 6.0,
  8.3, 10.0 and 10.8, and p.9 mentions pH 4.3 and 6.0 as the best for TNT while p.8 gives 10.3 and 4.8 as
  the best for 4-ABT.
- p.4 states the TNT concentrations 1.0e-4, 1.0e-6 and 1.0e-10 M with 2.5 uL aliquots, p.9 uses "3 μl of
  a solution of the explosive at 1.17 mm", p.10 uses 2 uL.
- p.10 gives the TNT mass in the interrogated area as 7.8e-12 g, p.11 concludes "we have detected
  7.8 × 10−18 g of tnt".
- p.9 uses a 10x objective with a ~13 um2 spot, p.10 a 20x objective with 2.3e3 um2 — the higher
  magnification is assigned the larger spot.
- p.4 "the nps used were prepared in acetone to facilitate the evaporation of the solvent" — acetone is
  not among the solvents of Section 2.1 (deionized water only); acetonitrile and isopropanol appear only
  in the abstract.
- p.1 abstract "the average characteristic (longer) size of the nps obtained ranged from 3 to 70 nm"
  against Table 1 (71-96 nm) and Table 2 (47-96 nm).
- p.5 "the best enhancement in the raman signals for 4-abt was observed at 20 min of irra diation time for
  ag and au nps" while Figure 3a is described as BPE on Au NPs.
