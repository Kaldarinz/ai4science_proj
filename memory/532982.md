## Verdict: no claim

The article performs one synthesis at one fixed laser setting and varies nothing in
the laser: Table 1 (p.2) gives repetition rate 100 kHz, pulse energy 30 µJ, wavelength
1030 nm, pulse duration 250 fs, F-theta lens with 100 mm focal length, scanning speed
4 m/s. The only size variation in the article is produced by centrifugation
fractionation (p.3–4), i.e. by post-processing, not by a laser variable. The
spectrophotometric method is calibrated against TEM size, so the reported dependences
are concentration-vs-optical-density and diameter-vs-A_PRP/A_400 — neither involves
fluence, energy, power or intensity.

Keyword sweep over the whole main PDF: "fluence" occurs only inside "influence"
(p.4); "power" does not occur at all; "intensity" occurs only in the Beer–Lambert
definition of light intensity I0/I (p.2); "energy" occurs only as "Pulse energy 30 μJ"
in Table 1 (p.2); "increase" occurs only for the optical density after BSA addition
(p.4). No J/cm², mJ/cm², W or W/cm² value appears anywhere. Hence the claims_spec §2
condition for an unnamed-"laser parameters" claim (of the laser parameters, only
fluence/energy/power/intensity varied) is not met.

## Rejected sentences and reasons

- p.1 abstract "The determination of the size of nanoparticles by the absorption
  spectra in the ultraviolet and visible regions is possible for particles with a
  size of at least 11 nm." — a validity range of the spectrophotometric method, not a
  dependence of size on a laser variable.
- p.1 "When scaling the production of gold nanoparticles to an industrial level, the
  laser ablation method is more cost effective [10]." — cost comparison, cited.
- p.1 "However, when obtaining gold nanoparticles by the laser ablation method, the
  maximum concentration of gold in the final colloidal solution of nanoparticles is
  limited by the optical permeability of the solution itself for the ablating laser
  and is approximately ~0.1 mg/mL." — concentration limit set by solution
  transparency; no size statement and no varied laser variable.
- p.2 "The Mie theory of light scattering and absorption on spherical nanoparticles
  determines relation of PRP in the absorption spectrum and nanoparticles' size
  [16]." — size-vs-optical-property relation, cited; not a laser variable.
- p.2 "The laser parameters, which was used for the synthesis, are indicated in
  Table 1." — points to a single fixed parameter set; states no size dependence.
- p.3 "The mass concentration of gold determined in this way was 0.1 mg Au/mL." —
  concentration, not size.
- p.3 "To fractionate the initial solution by size, a 5427R centrifuge ... was used."
  — size separation by centrifugation; the varying factor is centrifugal acceleration,
  not a laser variable.
- p.4 "Gold nanoparticles mass concentration dependence on the optical density at
  400 nm wavelength was plotted." and the calibration equation — concentration vs
  optical density.
- p.4 "To define dependence of the optical densities ratio at the wavelengths of the
  PRP and 400 nm (APRP/A400 nm) from nanoprticles size ..." — optical ratio vs size,
  the inverse of the calibration; no laser variable.
- p.4 "The obtained results are in good agreement for the sizes of nanoparticles more
  than 11 nm." / p.5 "A discrepancy between sizes determined by different methods is
  observed when the size of gold nanoparticles is less than 11 nm." — method
  agreement, not a laser dependence.
- p.4 Fig. 5 caption "The dependence of the average diameter of gold nanoparticles on
  the ratio of optical densities at the wavelength of the PRP and 400 nm." — caption
  alone, and the abscissa is an optical ratio.
- p.5 conclusions "Regardless of the synthesis method, the nanoparticles have similar
  optical properties ..." — method comparison, no laser variable.
