## Verdict: no claim
- The article holds the laser variable fixed and varies the solution chemistry. p.1: "The ablation of
  gold was carried out at a fixed fluence in 10 mM a-CD, at pH 3, 5, 6, and 9"; SI p.1: "the laser
  fluence on the target surface was about 600 J/cm2". The size effect reported is a pH effect
  (about 2.5 nm with dispersion <=1.5 nm FWHM at pH 6 and 9, 20 nm at pH 3, p.1, Figure 1) plus a
  cyclodextrin-type effect (alpha-CD > gamma-CD > beta-CD, p.2).
- Keyword sweep over both normalized PDFs: "fluence" 2 hits on main p.1 (the fixed-fluence sentence
  and "influence"), 1 hit in SI p.1 (the 600 J/cm2 value); "power" 0 hits; "intensity" main p.1 once
  (fs-versus-ns advantage sentence) and SI p.2 once (XPS axis label); "energy" main p.1 (max 1 mJ/pulse
  is in the SI; main p.1 has "low energy to minimize the Joule heating effect"), main p.2 ("total energy
  of the system"), SI p.1 twice (1 mJ/pulse, XPS pass energy 20 eV), SI p.2 once (binding energy axis).
  No J/cm2 series, no mJ series, no W or W/cm2 value anywhere.
- One no_claims row quotes the fixed-fluence sentence (main p.1): it names fluence only as a constant
  and reports no size value or size change with a laser variable.

## Rejected sentences and why
- p.1 "This approach offers two key advantages over nanosecond laser ablation: inherent high intensity
  and low energy to minimize the Joule heating effect." — compares pulse-duration regimes (fs vs ns),
  names intensity and energy without values or units, and states no nanoparticle-size dependence; the
  stated benefit is reduced Joule heating.
- p.1 "alpha-CD is most effective in the size control, resulting in monodispersed colloidal particles
  (2 to 2.5 nm)." — size control by the cyclodextrin type, not by a laser variable.
- p.1 "we recently reported a significant improvement in size reduction and stability of colloidal gold
  nanoparticles produced by femtosecond laser ablation in alpha-cyclodextrin (CD), alpha-CD, and
  gamma-CD.12" — cited (ref 12) and attributes the effect to the CD, not to fluence/energy/power/intensity.
- p.1 "however, the laser ablation of metals in deionized water generally results in rather large
  (20-300 nm) and highly disperse (50-300 nm) nanoparticles because of the postablation coalescence of
  nanoclusters.5,12" — general statement about the medium (water) with no laser variable and no direction
  with respect to fluence/energy/power/intensity.
- p.1 "At high pH values (pH 6 and 9), the mean particle size was about 2.5 nm and the size dispersion did
  not exceed 1.5 nm FWHM (supporting information)." and "In contrast, particles prepared at low pHs were
  much larger (20 nm at pH 3) with broader size dispersions" — size values at one fixed fluence, varying pH.
- p.1 "In these cases, the mean size and dispersion were almost independent of the pH value (Figure 1)." —
  a no-effect statement about pH, not about a laser variable.
- p.2 "the formation of O- takes place only at relatively high pH > 5, which corresponds to the reduction of
  gold nanoparticle size in our experiments" and the hydrogen-bonding/charge-repulsion mechanism sentences —
  mechanism explanations for the pH dependence, no laser variable.
- p.2 "such a shift is generally attributed to the increase in the strength of intermolecular hydrogen
  bonding.19" — FT-IR band shift, not particle size.
- p.2 "alpha-CD is capable of forming much stronger hydrophobic complexes with metallic gold to minimize the
  total energy of the system" — thermodynamic statement, not a laser variable.
- Figure 1 and Figure 2 captions (size/dispersion versus pH; zeta-potential versus pH) — captions alone are
  excluded by claims_spec section 2, and their abscissa is pH.
- SI p.1 TEM distributions at pH 9 and pH 3 — the varied parameter is pH at the single 600 J/cm2 fluence.
