# 537549 — Palazzo et al., J. Colloid Interface Sci. 489 (2017) 47–56

"On the stability of gold nanoparticles synthesized by laser ablation in liquids". Main PDF 10 pp,
no supplied SI.

## Evidence locations (laser and size)

- p.2, §2.1: single laser setting — "nanoseconds Nd-YAG laser (Quanta System Thunder) operating with
  fundamental (1064 nm), having a laser frequency of 10 Hz and a nominal pulse duration of 8 ns".
- p.3: "the irradiance was of 71 GW/cm2, and the laser-ablation time of 6 min"; 25 ml of water or salt
  solution, 5 cm focal lens, 99.999% Au pellet. No second laser setting anywhere in the article.
- p.3: "When the ablation parameters are kept fixed, the nature of the liquid phase has a small
  influence on the size and concentration of the AuNP and the whole set of AuNPs examined in this work
  presents an average diameter of 13 ± 3 nm, and concentration of 0.08 ± 0.02 mg/mL."
- p.5–6, §4: the varied variable is solution pH/composition — 10^-4 M HCl, KCl, NaOH (Fig. 3), acids
  HCl/H2SO4/HNO3 at pH 3 (Table 1), buffers at pH 3–10. Size reported as invariant: SPR peak near
  520 nm "indicative of small NPs with a radius of the metallic core (rc) around 5 nm" (p.5);
  Table 1 rc = 5–6 ± 3 nm, rh = 6–7 ± 1–3 nm.
- p.9, §5 conclusions: "under all the conditions the size of the particles was close to 5 nm in radius";
  the pH effect is on the zeta-potential (about 30 mV from pH 3 to pH 10), not on size.

## Verdict

No claim. The laser is held at one fixed setting; the varied knobs are solution pH and anion
composition. No sentence states that Au particle size changes, or does not change, with fluence,
energy, power or intensity. One no-claim row quotes the p.3 sentence, which is the closest approach:
it reports a size value and a size (in)dependence, but on the liquid phase, with the laser parameters
explicitly fixed.

## Rejected sentences and reasons

- p.2: "in general, a fair comparison among these different results is complicated because the
  experiments have been performed using different setups and it is known that the ablation laser pulse
  duration, fluence and wavelength all impacts the mechanism of NPs production." — names fluence but
  speaks of the mechanism of NP production, not of size, and states no direction; general statement,
  no citation attached to the dependence.
- p.5: "the difference in the surface chemical composition is likely due to the differences in
  ablation laser pulse, fluence and wavelength but remains the fact that the stability of the NPs is
  unaffected by the presence gold oxides on the surface of NPs." — the laser variables explain surface
  chemistry, not size; no size statement.
- p.6: "when the number of ions becomes negligible because of plasma recombination and particle
  formation, the size of the NP depends on the plasma temperature as a consequence of the
  evaporation/condensation equilibrium" — plasma temperature, not a laser variable; mechanism.
- p.2: "when the laser pulse of irradiance between 0.1 and 10 GW cm-2 irradiate the metal target in
  bulk liquid, breakdown process and plasma generation take place" — plasma generation, no size.
- p.7: "a crucial point is that the colloidal size of the NP maintains the energy of the electrons
  small enough to preclude chemical reactions" — size as a condition for the charging model, not a
  dependence of size on a laser variable.
- p.7: "the kinetics seem to depend on several variables such as the starting f-potential of the AuNPs,
  and the size of the bulk gold" — aggregation kinetics, not NP size; not a laser variable.
- p.9 conclusions: "the main effect of the pH of the solution was on the measured f-potential" — pH
  effect on zeta-potential, not size, and pH is not a laser variable.
- Fig. 3, 5, 6, 8, 9 captions — captions alone are excluded by claims_spec §2; they show pH and
  grounding-time dependences, not laser-variable dependences.

## Keyword sweep (whole PDF, normalized text)

"fluence" 4x: p.2 (mechanism sentence above), p.3 (ablation parameters kept fixed), p.5 (surface
composition sentence), p.10 (reference [15] title "influence"). "power" 2x: p.1 and p.9, both
"oxidation power" of the acids. "intensity" 1x: p.3, autocorrelation function of scattered light
intensity. "irradiance" 2x: p.2 (general 0.1–10 GW/cm2 range) and p.3 (fixed 71 GW/cm2). "energy"
only as Born energy / free energy / electron energy. No J/cm2, mJ, uJ, W or W/cm2 value anywhere.

None open. The article is a single-laser-setting stability study; its only size statements concern
solution pH/composition, which is outside the four laser variables.
