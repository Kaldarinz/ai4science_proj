## Verdict: one claim, basis `cited`
- `537967-c1` — p.4, section 3.2 discussion of the gold colloid: "As has already been reported, laser fluence,
  inter-pulse irradiation and the presence of surfactants or stabilizing agents in the liquid can determine the
  final particle size [6]." Variable `fluence` (named, no unit in the sentence, claims_spec §5), direction
  `affects` (no direction stated), basis `cited` (ref. 6 = Kabashin & Meunier, J. Appl. Phys. 94 (2003) 7941),
  material Au, medium_class `water` (deionized water, no additives: "The same fluence with no chemical additives
  was used for the generation process").
- Material is contextual, not written in the sentence: the paragraph is about this work's gold colloid ("the
  nanoparticles formed in the liquid by laser ablation", "the differences in the particle size"), so "the final
  particle size" is the gold particle size. Flagged for the curator (question 1).

## No own-data claim: the varied laser parameter is the repetition rate
- p.2: "The repetition rate was varied from 5000 Hz to 100 Hz, while the pulse energy was kept constant (240 uJ)."
  Fluence is constant ("The same fluence with no chemical additives was used for the generation process", p.4);
  no fluence, power or intensity value is reported anywhere in the article.
- The own-data dependence is stated on the repetition rate (Hz), which is not fluence, energy, power or intensity
  (claims_spec §2, §5): p.3 "The hydrodynamic diameter decreases with increasing repetition rate (Fig. 3a)"; p.3
  "the Z-average, i.e. the intensity-weighted mean hydrodynamic diameter of the nanoparticles, and the
  corresponding mean diameter of the number-weighted distribution decreases when the repetition rate is
  increased"; abstract p.1 "The increment of the repetition rate does not influence the ablation efficiency, but
  produces a decrease of the hydrodynamic diameter and blue-shift of the plasmon resonance of the generated gold
  nanoparticles"; p.6 "The pulse repetition rate was shown to control the colloidal hydrodynamic size
  distribution". The §2 clause for unnamed "laser parameters" does not apply: the parameter is named and is not
  one of the four.
- Average power does scale with the repetition rate at constant pulse energy (p.1: "Since laser power scales with
  the repetition rate (at constant pulse energy)"), so the own series is physically a size-versus-average-power
  series (24 mW at 100 Hz to 1.2 W at 5000 Hz). The text never states the dependence in power units and reports no
  W value, so §5 (decide by the unit of the quantity the text varies) gives no `power` claim. Flagged for the
  curator (question 2).

## Rejected sentences and why
- p.1 "It has been reported that laser parameters, such as fluence [6] and wavelength [7] affect the final size
  distribution." — cited, but generic: the paragraph is about "nanoparticles from the laser-ablated material" in
  general, no gold or gold-containing material is named, so claims_spec §2's material condition is not met.
- p.1 "For example, bimodal size particle distributions are formed if high fluences are used for the ablation of
  solid targets in liquids [8]." — cited, generic material, and it states the formation of a bimodal distribution,
  not a change of nanoparticle size with fluence.
- p.1 "The control of nanoparticle size, and hence colloidal properties, is possible using laser fluence, but this
  is detrimental concerning nanoparticle production rate." — generic (no material named), no direction, and it
  states a productivity trade-off rather than a size change with fluence.
- p.1 "However, narrow and small particle size distributions occur during the ablation process when the laser pulse
  energy is absorbed efficiently by the particles[10]." — the pulse energy is not varied; the sentence is about
  absorption by the particles, not a size-versus-energy dependence.
- p.1 "Nevertheless, particles dispersed in the liquid scatter and absorb the incoming laser pulses, causing
  different yields, depending on the particle size or material, and the laser wavelength or intensity." — yields,
  not size; no size dependence stated.
- p.2 "Since absorption of laser energy by the colloid can affect nanoparticle shape and size, additional
  investigations on the quality of the colloid have been carried out." — motivation for the post-irradiation
  experiments; no laser variable is varied and no direction is stated.
- p.3 "Fig. 2a shows that the gold colloid generated at high repetition rates (5000 Hz) has a bimodal distribution
  with a population of small dispersed particles (15 nm) and large particles/agglomerates (50 nm). In the
  colloidal sample produced at low repetition rates (100 Hz), high hydrodynamic sizes are dominant." — two values
  at two repetition rates; the dependence is on the repetition rate, and the reader's comparison of the values is
  data, not a claim (claims_spec §2).
- p.3 "The shift to longer wavelengths of the plasmon peak maxima indicates that larger particles and more
  agglomerates result when low repetition rates are applied during the laser ablation process." — repetition rate,
  and the size statement is inferred from the plasmon shift.
- p.4 "During the ablation process in liquids, nanoparticles are generated and simultaneously irradiated. ...
  As the laser pulse frequency increases, the fragmentation rate increases, and for this reason at equal ablation
  times, smaller nanoparticles are generated." — pulse frequency, not one of the four variables.
- p.4 "We can conclude that the post-irradiation process is more efficient at low repetition rates, since a lower
  number of pulses are required to produce the same hydrodynamic size reduction effect in the colloid." —
  fragmentation efficiency versus repetition rate.
- p.5 "In the inter-pulse temporal range investigated (200 us to 10 ms), shielding effects in the ablation process
  can be neglected, but post-irradiation at different repetition rates strongly affects the final hydrodynamic
  particle size distribution." — repetition rate.
- p.5 "Colloidal phenomena involved in the millisecond time scale are usually driven by diffusion in the liquid
  [34], as measured for the case of gold nanoparticles in optical tweezers at low powers ..." — "low powers" is a
  condition of a cited diffusion measurement, not a size-versus-power dependence.
- p.5-6 conclusions ("the temporal inter-pulse distance ... plays a significant role in the final hydrodynamic
  particle size distribution"; "At repetition rates over 500 Hz, fragmentation efficiency is constant") —
  inter-pulse distance and repetition rate.
- Figure captions (Fig. 1 productivity/efficiency, Fig. 2 hydrodynamic diameter and plasmon band at 5000 and
  100 Hz, Fig. 3 hydrodynamic diameter/agglomeration index/plasmon peak at variable repetition rates, Figs. 4-6

## Questions for the curator
1. The single claim's material is contextual (the sentence says "the final particle size" inside the gold-colloid
   paragraph) and its basis is `cited` to ref. 6. If the sentence is read as a generic statement about laser
   ablation in liquids, the claim must be dropped and replaced by a no-claim row quoting it; the article would
   then have no claim at all.
2. The own experiments vary the repetition rate at constant pulse energy, i.e. they vary the average power
   (24 mW to 1.2 W) without ever stating a power value or a size-versus-power dependence. Under claims_spec §5
   this is not a `power` claim; if the curator prefers to treat the repetition-rate series as a power series, the
   article would gain one `own_data` claim (direction `decrease`) and the comparison stage would need candidates.
