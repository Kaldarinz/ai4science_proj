## Verdict: one own-data claim
- `535841-c1` — p.3, results section: "The hypothesis on the fluence dependence of the nanoparticle size
  was confirmed by TEM data, which revealed a drastic particle size reduction under the laser fluence
  decrease." Direction `increase` (size falls as fluence falls), variable `fluence` (J/cm2 throughout,
  claims_spec §5), basis `own_data`, material Au, medium_class `water` (pure 18 MOhm deionized water,
  no additives), conditions fs pulses 110 fs / 800 nm / 1 kHz, F 60-1000 J/cm2.
- Variable decision, textual support: p.3 "identical focusing conditions were maintained by fixing both
  the target-lens distance and the water thickness, while the radiation energy was the main variable
  parameter to control the laser fluence" — the varied knob is pulse energy, but every reported value and
  every dependence is expressed in J/cm2, so the unit rule gives `fluence`, not `energy`.
- Restatements of the same claim (same material/medium/variable/direction/basis, so not separate rows):
  abstract p.2 ("manifests itself at relatively low laser fluences F<400 J/cm2 and leads to very small
  (3-10 nm) ... colloids. The second one ... takes place at high fluences and gives rise to a much larger
  particle size"); p.3 "Both distributions demonstrated an increase of the mean size and dispersion while
  the fluence increased"; p.3 "the broad distribution showed a much stronger and almost linear increase of
  these characteristics as the fluence increased"; p.3 "the mean particle size dropped from 120 to 4 nm as
  F decreased from 1000 to 60 J/cm2". Quote taken from the results section per claims_spec §2.

## Rejected sentences and why
- p.2 "although certain size control can be achieved by a variation of radiation parameters.12,16" —
  the sentence names no material (generic "particles"/colloidal metal nanoparticles in the preceding
  sentence), states no direction, and "size control" is not a size change with a named laser variable;
  the Au-specific statements in that paragraph concern surfactants/cyclodextrins, not radiation parameters.
- p.3 "The plasma plume intensity depended on the laser energy and light focusing conditions." — plume
  property, no particle size.
- p.3 "This peak was around 520-530 nm for F<100 J/cm2, which was consistent with the presence of small
  3-30 nm particles" and "for F>100 J/cm2, the peak suffered a broadening and a red shift to 540-550 nm,
  suggesting a certain ''dephasing'' ... due to the increase of particle size and dispersion" — optical
  property; the size increase is inferred from the spectrum, not stated as a fluence dependence (and it
  would be the same claim as c1 anyway).
- p.3 "the peak related to the narrow distribution could be discerned only at F<400 J/cm2, while the broad
  distribution-related peak was absent at F<100 J/cm2" — presence/absence of distribution components, no
  size direction.
- p.3 "the increase rate was not very significant for the narrow distribution with ranges of variations
  for the mean size and dispersion from 4 to 2 nm and from 2 to 17 nm" — still an increase, only weaker;
  not a `no_effect` claim, and the same (material, medium, variable, direction, basis) as c1.
- p.4 "The absence of the heat-affected zone for low fluences ... gives evidence that origin of the narrow
  distribution is related to the radiative ablation of the target" and "the target is heated by the plasma
  itself" — mechanism explanations with no stated size-fluence dependence.
- p.4 "The surprisingly small mean size (4 nm) and dispersion (2 nm at FWHM) of particles [Fig. 1(c)] are
  probably granted by the absence of target or particle heating effects" — single value at one fluence,
  mechanism attribution.
- p.4 "the tendency of the nanoparticle size reduction with the laser fluence decrease was also mentioned
  under the nanosecond laser ablation of Ag in aqueous SDS solutions.13" — cited and about Ag, not Au.
- p.4 summary "We revealed two different nanoparticle distributions, suggesting contributions of different
  mechanisms of material ablation." — no size-fluence dependence.
- Figure captions (Fig. 1 fluence labels, Fig. 2 "as a function of laser fluence", Fig. 3 crater fluences)
  — captions alone are excluded by claims_spec §2.
