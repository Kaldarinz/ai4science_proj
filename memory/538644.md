## Verdict: no claim

The article varies **no laser parameter**. Materials and Methods (p.7) states one fixed setting:
"A Litron Q-switching Nd:YAG laser [27] operating at the 1064 nm wavelength, with a 5 ns pulse duration,
200 mJ pulse energy, 1 mm2 focused spot, and 10 Hz pulse repetition rate, was employed." The irradiation
lasts 5 min (p.7). The only varied factor is the liquid medium: 8 mL distilled water (suspension 1) vs
8 mL curing agent (suspension 2), p.7. All size differences reported (p.3, p.5, p.6, p.9) follow that
medium, never a fluence, energy, power or intensity change.

Keyword sweep over the normalized pages (checker's pypdf extractor):
- "fluence" — only in the p.2 cited sentence "Several studies [20] confirm that the critical parameter
  for laser ablation in medium, more than laser fluence, wavelength, and pulse duration, is environment."
  No fluence value in J/cm2 or mJ/cm2 anywhere in the article.
- "energy" — the single 200 mJ pulse energy (p.7), "energy dispersive X-ray (EDX)" (p.1, p.8, p.9),
  "surface energy" (p.4), "energy gap" in reference 21 (p.11).
- "power" — absent.
- "intensity" — only SPR peak intensity (p.3, p.6).
- "irradiance" — absent. No W/cm2 or GW/cm2 value anywhere.

So the claims_spec §2 condition for an unnamed "laser parameters" claim (of the laser parameters, only
fluence/energy/power/intensity varied) is not met: nothing is varied.

## Rejected sentences and reasons

- p.2 "Several studies [20] confirm that the critical parameter for laser ablation in medium, more than
  laser fluence, wavelength, and pulse duration, is environment." — cited, about the ablation process,
  not about NP size; it ranks environment above fluence but states no size dependence and no direction.
- p.2 "It is well-known that the ablation rate decreases with increasing medium density and boiling
  point [21]." — ablation rate vs medium density, not size vs a laser variable; cited.
- p.2 "It also decreases with increasing level of liquid medium, which should be crossed by the laser
  beam before it hits the solid target, leading to a reduction of the number of produced nanoparticles."
  — number of particles (yield), not size; the varied quantity is the liquid level.
- p.3 "The position and width of the SPR absorption peak depends on both the size and agglomeration of
  the gold NPs." — optical property vs size, not size vs laser variable.
- p.3 "confirming the formation of a spherical gold nanoparticle with a size of about 20 nm [22]" — one
  size at one setting, cited; no dependence stated.
- p.3 "The suspension of the curing agent containing the AuNPs shows higher absorbance due to the
  presence of bigger-in-size AuNP nanostructures (as can be seen in Figure 3c) and of carbon groups
  generated during the laser irradiation as shown in Figure 2—blue curve 2." — size difference between
  media, and absorbance vs size; "during the laser irradiation" names no variable and no direction.
- p.5 "The AuNPs average size is about ~20 nm in water (a,b) and hundreds of nm in the curing agent."
  — the no-claim quote: a size difference between the two media at the same fixed laser setting.
- p.6 "The SPR band is affected by the size of the NPs, and a slight red shift is observed in Suspension

- p.6 "The broader band in curing agent +AuNPs indicates wider size distribution." — width vs medium.
- p.6 "The lower SPR band intensity in Suspension 2 than in Suspension 1 can be explained by a lower

- p.6 "This minor AuNPs amount is due to the lowest plasma plume expansion velocity in this medium

- p.7 "suggesting that the higher viscosity of the liquid polymer, with respect to the water, can be

- p.9 "The suspension of distilled water and AuNPs is ruby red due to the presence of spherical gold

- p.9 "These larger Au nanostructures can be due to the lower expansion velocity of the plasma plume in

## Textual inconsistencies recorded for the curator

- p.7 states both "1 mm2 focused spot" and, two sentences later, "a 50 cm focal length convergent lens
  to obtain a 1 mm spot diameter" — area vs diameter for the same spot.
- p.7 gives the AuNP concentration as "about 0.9 mg/mL in both the distilled water and the curing
  agent", while p.3 gives 1.1e13 NPs/cm3 for the water suspension; the two are not reconciled.
- p.5 "The AuNPs average size is about ~20 nm in water (a,b) and hundreds of nm in the curing agent"
  against p.3 "a size of about 20 nm [22]" (cited) and p.9 "about 20 nm" — consistent for water, but the
  curing-agent size is never quantified beyond "hundreds of nm".
- p.5 "The NPs have a size distribution with a deviation of about 10%." — no N, no measurement method
  for the deviation.
- p.6 Table 1 lists Au at 1.51 atom % in the water suspension but the text says "a lower concentration
  (0.07 atom %) of AuNPs in the suspension containing the curing agent" — the comparison is between
  suspensions, and the table's Si column (80.30 vs 52.37) dominates the normalization.
- p.4 "The electrical conductivity σ for the bulk PDMS is very low, about 2.5 × 10-16 (Ω·cm)-1, as
  reported in literature [24]" — ref. 24 is a web page, not a journal article.
- p.2, p.3, p.4, p.5, p.7, p.8 carry duplicated "x FOR PEER REVIEW" overlays of the same text, so the
  extracted text of those pages contains each sentence twice; this is a layout artifact, not a second
  statement of the claim.
- p.8 "Samples were coated with a layer of Pt with thickness less than 50 μm" — implausible for a
  sputter coating (likely nm).
