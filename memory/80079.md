## Fixed laser parameters, varied parameter
- p.3: Yb:KGW, 1030 nm, 270 fs, power 705 mW, fluence 2.5 J/cm2, 10 kHz, beam 60 um.
- The only varied quantity is the scanning speed, 870 vs 3840 mm/s (spot shift of 0.5 vs 2 beam diameters),
  i.e. pulse overlap and the resulting target porosity. No fluence, energy, power or intensity is varied.
- Size results (p.3, p.7-p.8): 870 mm/s -> 1-11 nm, local maximum 4 nm, unimodal; 3840 mm/s -> 1-40 nm,
  bimodal with maxima 5 and 25 nm. These are a scanning-speed/porosity dependence, not a fluence dependence.

## Verdict: one claim, basis `general`
- `80079-c1`, p.8 (conclusions): "This mechanism can be achieved at high fluence regime to generate a
  significant amount of vapor, which leads to the formation of fine NPs population during the nucleation
  process." The sentence states that the high-fluence regime produces the fine (small-size) population, i.e.
  a size-fluence dependence with a direction; `variable=fluence` (J/cm2 throughout the article),
  `direction=decrease` (higher fluence -> finer/smaller particles), `material=Au`, `medium_class=water`
  (deionized water experiments, water ambient in the simulations).
- Basis `general`: no citation in the sentence and no own data at more than one fluence; it is the authors'
  mechanistic conclusion from a single-fluence simulation plus literature reasoning. It is not `own_data`
  because the article never varies fluence.

## Rejected sentences and why
- p.8 "These connections give us a possibility for the manipulation of size characteristics of formed NPs'
  via the variation of laser irradiation parameters." — unnamed laser parameters; under claims_spec §2 such a
  sentence is a claim only when, of the laser parameters, the article varies only fluence, energy, power or
  intensity. Here none of them is varied (only scanning speed), so the condition fails.
- p.8 "Although it was not possible to completely suppress or isolate one or another channel ..., one can
  experimentally tune the regimes of laser irradiation, which can provide the unimodal size distribution of
  NPs of spherical shape." — same reason: unnamed irradiation parameters, no direction, and the tuned
  parameter in this work is the scanning speed.
- p.8 "While the effect of pulse duration and the absorbed fluence on the NPs formation process in liquids
  has been recently studied, ..." — states that the effect has been studied, not a size-fluence dependence;
  no citation number and no direction.
- p.7 "The former can be activated by means of increasing of both the pulse duration and the incident fluence
  [50,51,52]" — cited statement about activating the thermal-confinement regime, not about particle size; the
  size link is only in the following sentence (recorded as `c1`).
- p.5 "The latter one gives us the possibility to manipulate with the amount of heat dissipated through this
  channel (the electron-phonon coupling) via varying the laser fluence, and thus to control the amount of heat
  dissipated through the other channel (the electron thermal conductivity)." — heat-dissipation channels, no
  size statement.
- p.2 "Although the second coarser population could be discriminated by a proper selection of laser parameters
  [21,32,39]" — cited, unnamed laser parameters, no direction, and the article varies no such parameter.
- p.2 "We show that depending on the absence or presence of this subsurface porosity, the dissipation of the
  deposited laser energy can follow different channels, which results in the appearance of either bimodal or
  monomodal size distributions of formed NPs." — porosity/scanning-speed dependence, not a laser variable.
- p.1 abstract and p.2 introduction sentences on controlling NP size by fs PLAL — no laser variable named and
  no direction; the article varies no fluence/energy/power/intensity.
- p.3 "In the case when the laser beam shifted by half the diameter with the beam speed 870 mm/sec ... the
  size of the obtained nanoparticles lay in the narrow range of 1-11 nm ..." and the 3840 mm/sec counterpart —
  reported values at two scanning speeds; a dependence the reader infers by comparing values is data, not a
  claim (claims_spec §2), and the varied quantity is not a laser parameter.
- Fig. 5 caption ("at the incident fluence of 2.5 mJ/cm2") — caption alone, and the unit there (mJ/cm2)
  conflicts with the 2.5 J/cm2 of the text; noted as a typo, not a claim.
